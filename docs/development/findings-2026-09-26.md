# Findings — 2026-09-26 (transcription quality, from real calls)

Everything here came from the interpreter pasting two real transcripts, not from
a ticket. Three real defects, one blind spot, and one process incident.

---

## 1. Words were stranded live at the end of every turn (FIXED, v4.160.0)

**Evidence:** a call where the bubble ended `…My last name? Okay. Yeah. That he has`.

**What happened:** when a final payload sentence-splits, the trailing incomplete
sentence becomes a LIVE row on purpose (v4.141.0 reuses the live draft's id so
the bubble grows without remounting). But **nothing ever sealed it** — the next
turn started a new row and left the old one live forever. Those words never
entered the sealed transcript and never got translated.

The app's own diagnostic said it out loud and nobody read it:

```
[CAT VANISH] caption_bubble_split → lost: ['Yeah.']
```

**Fix:** on a turn transition, a live row that still holds words is sealed first.
The live-tail design is untouched. `flagVanish` was also lying (it reported the
tail as "lost"), which is how a real vanishing-phone-number alarm gets ignored.

**The lesson that matters:** the metric said `damage 0.00` and 1315 tests were
green while this was losing real words in production. A fixture corpus cannot
catch anything that depends on real audio timing.

---

## 2. A translation silently dropped its last clause (FIXED, v4.161.0)

**Evidence:** a CSA call. Source ended `…he already has those resources there
waiting for him.` Translation ended `…Él ya tiene esos recursos allí`.

Also `after all of this is done` → `después de todo de esto se hace` — wrong
sense ("is made", not "is finished").

**Why nothing caught it:** `isSuspiciouslyShort` only fires under 25% of source
words, and the Spanish came back *longer* (31 words vs 26). Not blank, not
passthrough, no digits lost. **The safety net guarded against absence and had
nothing at all against silent omission** — which is the dangerous one, because a
fluent translation gets trusted.

**Fix:** `isTruncatedTranslation()` — the source dangles a modifier ("…waiting for
him") AND the translation does not end in terminal punctuation. Both required, so
it cannot fire alone. 6 must-not-fire cases against 2 must-fire.

**Still true:** a detector, not a fixer. No heuristic proves meaning is complete.
For terms that must land every time, the **glossary** is the real tool.

## 3. Phrase-level glossary, operator's choice (v4.163.0)

Sentence-exact pinning only fires on one exact sentence, so CSA work meant
re-pinning the same term by hand in every new phrasing. Now:

- **OFF** — pins ignored · **EXACT** — one exact sentence (default, unchanged) ·
  **PHRASE** — pinned terms applied inside every future sentence
- longest pin wins; word boundaries; accents folded; per-lane only
- a pin qualifies as a phrase if it is 2+ words, or 1 word of ≥4 chars, so
  `disclosure` works while `no` and `the` are refused
- Settings → Translation now LISTS every pin with a delete button. The store used
  to be invisible, which made it impossible to audit what the app was forcing.

## 4. OPEN: CONNECT needs a Zap to work — needs evidence before any change

**Symptom:** press CONNECT, nothing usable; press Zap, it works.

**What Zap does that the first press does not** (`reconnectStream`, line 2301):
bumps `connectAttemptIdRef`, `closeConnections()`, `clearWatchdog()`, waits
**400 ms**, then reuses the existing stream (`streamRef.current`) without
re-resolving the source or re-prompting for permission.

**What the first press does** (`startRecording`, line 1955): bumps the attempt id,
resolves the source, tries to REUSE the stream, and only then `startDeepgram`.

**Most likely mechanism (unverified):** the 12 s stall watchdog
(`watchdogTimeoutRef`, line 962). `connectStallVerdict()` sees
`audioChunksSent === 0` and returns `fail-silent`, so the app declares TIMEOUT
before tab audio has had any chance to start. By the time the user presses Zap the
stream is warm, Zap reuses it, and it works. This is consistent with every symptom
but it is **a hypothesis, not a finding**.

**Known dead code found on the way:** `shouldHealConnect` is imported at line 56
and **never used** (confirmed by the build's `no-unused-vars` warning). The
v4.153.0 note "1.5 s after a press with no audio it self-heals once" describes
mitigation that is not wired in.

**Deliberately NOT changed.** `useDeepgram.js` is 2300+ lines of race-prone
lifecycle code, and the symptom has at least three plausible causes. Guessing here
could make every call worse. What is needed is one paste from the failing press:

```js
__CAT_DUMP('[Deepgram')        // every socket open/close/stall in the ring
__catintSttTrace.slice(-40)    // stage-by-stage trace of the attempt
```

plus the `[CAT STT CRIT]` console lines, which already carry `attemptId`. That
names the failing step exactly.

## 5. PROCESS: two agents on one working tree destroyed work twice

The other session's `git add -A` + `commit --amend` + a `git clean` wiped five
in-progress files off disk mid-task. They were recovered from the reflog
(pre-amend commit `f57aaac`) and re-verified before recommitting.

tree.** One session's `clean` is another session's lost afternoon.
